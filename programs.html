The programs, in detail

Four programs of 4 to 12 weeks, taught on your flour, your kitchen and your oven.

Program 01 · From flour to bread

Starter & first loaf

Most starters are not dead, they are cold or hungry. We set a feeding rhythm you can keep, learn the signs of a starter at its peak, and walk through a first loaf end to end — mixing, bulk, shaping, baking — with a photo review at each stage.

  • 8 weeks
  • Complete beginners
  • 3 hours a week
  • Flour, water, a jar

Program 02 · Open, even, not gummy

Crumb & shaping

A dense loaf, a gummy center and a flat pancake each have specific causes, and a photo of the crumb usually names them. We work on judging fermentation by the dough rather than the clock, shaping with real tension, and scoring that opens rather than tears.

  • 10 weeks
  • Baked a few loaves
  • 4 hours a week
  • A banneton helps

Start this program

Program 03 · Bread around a job

Schedules that fit

The fridge is the tool that makes home baking practical. We build schedules around your working week — mix in the evening, retard overnight, bake before work — and cover how temperature changes every timing in the recipe, summer and winter.

  • 6 weeks
  • Can bake a loaf
  • 3 hours a week
  • Fridge space

Start this program

Weeks 1–2 · Temperature

Dough temp, room temp, and adjusting timings.

Weeks 3–4 · Retarding

Weeks 5–6 · Your week

Weeks 1–2 · Heat and steam

Getting a crust from a domestic oven.

Weeks 3–4 · Whole grains

Hydration and handling when the flour changes.

Weeks 5–6 · Enriched

Butter, eggs and sugar, and how they slow things down.

Weeks 7–8 · Your repertoire

Three breads you can bake without thinking.

Program 04 · The last twenty minutes

Oven, crust & variations

Home ovens fight you on steam and on heat recovery. We work with what you own — Dutch oven, stone, pan of water — then branch into whole grain blends, enriched doughs and pan breads once the basic loaf is dependable.

  • 8 weeks
  • Reliable loaves
  • 4 hours a week
  • Your own oven
